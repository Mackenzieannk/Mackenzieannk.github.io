Process

HOW IT
WORKS.

Summit uses a two-tier structure — a feeder fund that aggregates smaller investor positions, which then contributes as a single institutional participant into the exchange fund. This is how we unlock access at $10K minimums.

The Feeder Structure

Traditional exchange funds require large individual contributions because the per-investor operational cost (legal, accounting, K-1 issuance, capital account tracking) is roughly fixed regardless of position size. Our feeder fund pools contributions across many smaller investors and enters the exchange fund as a single LP — absorbing that fixed cost collectively.

Tax Treatment

Contributions to a properly structured exchange fund are not considered a taxable event under IRC §721. Your contributed shares enter the fund at their original cost basis. After seven years, you withdraw a diversified basket of stocks — and pay no taxes until you eventually sell those holdings.

  • No capital gains tax at contribution
  • Original cost basis preserved through the fund
  • Taxes deferred until final sale of redeemed shares
  • Estate planning benefits: step-up in basis at death

The 20% Real Estate Requirement

IRS rules require that at least 20% of exchange fund assets be held in qualifying real property. We partner with an institutional real estate manager to deploy this sleeve — ensuring IRS compliance while maintaining institutional-grade quality of the non-equity holdings.

Monthly Closes

We target a monthly close cycle. You commit your shares, we verify eligibility and benchmark fit, and at close your position enters the fund. The entire digital enrollment process takes approximately 15 minutes.

The 7-Year Lock

Tax law requires a minimum 7-year holding period to withdraw a diversified basket. If you exit before 7 years, you receive your original stock back (not a diversified basket) and no taxable event has occurred. After 7 years, you may choose to redeem or remain invested.

Custody & Safety

All assets are custodied at a major institutional bank, independent of Summit. Independent fund administration and annual audits provide an additional layer of oversight. SIPC protection applies to brokerage accounts up to $500,000.

Eligibility

Participation in Summit Exchange Funds is limited to accredited investors as defined under Rule 501 of Regulation D of the Securities Act of 1933. An individual qualifies as an accredited investor if they have:

  • Net worth exceeding $1 million (excluding primary residence), individually or jointly with spouse
  • Annual income exceeding $200,000 (or $300,000 jointly) in each of the two most recent years
  • Certain professional certifications (Series 7, 65, or 82 license holders)
